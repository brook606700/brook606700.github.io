Day 3｜Setting Off in the Dark, Michelin Bites, and a Golden Church

May 23, 2026 · Zubiri → Pamplona · 20km

To grab albergue beds, we set off in the dark past four in the morning. The sky was still black, our headlamps the only light.

We barely spoke on the trail, but the glow of each other's lights kept us company through the darkness. Setting off before sunrise means you may miss some scenery along the way—but you will never miss that warm dawn and the sky full of color.

Today's route rolled up and down, through pleasant forest paths and little bridges over streams, then a hardcore uphill climb. Walking along, I actually spotted someone's lost slipper on the trail—yes, really, a slipper. I picked it up, and half an hour later a foreigner came walking back toward me with a "you just saved my life" look on his face. I knew I had found its owner, and in that instant we both burst out laughing.

This is probably the most charming thing about the Camino. The simplest gesture can bridge any language and bring people closer. On this road, there are really no strangers—only friends you have not met yet.

Entering the city, I stopped at Carrefour for a 1-liter glass bottle of beer—in the heat of the road, it was pure relief. I finally unlocked Spain's national dish, the Tortilla española. Found in nearly every bar, this soul food is an absolute must on the journey.

The albergue I reached at noon was a bit of a letdown—chaotic check-in left everyone waiting outside the door. Luckily it sits right by the river; opening the door to a lovely river view was its one redeeming grace.

Here we met a Taiwanese woman who had quit her job and been traveling the world for eight months. Listening to her story, her eyes shone with resilience. This road attracts not only travelers of the body, but explorers of the soul.

At last I arrived in Pamplona—the legendary city of bulls, and the first big city on the Camino. Switching from quiet towns through forest trails to noisy city streets, the sharp contrast genuinely felt like waking in another world. As Hemingway's land of inspiration, it is world-famous for the electric Running of the Bulls every July.

The city was wonderful to wander. I bought protein powder at Decathlon to speed up muscle recovery. Strolling downtown, I visited the towering cathedral—a solemn neoclassical facade wrapped around a magnificent Gothic interior. Passing the baroque city hall that ignites the Running of the Bulls opening revelry each year, I even happened upon an exciting triathlon on the way to the bull-running statue. The statue captures the most thrilling, highest-tension instant of the festival, letting travelers who cannot be there in July feel its wild soul firsthand.

Pushing open the doors of Café Iruña, the lavish vintage interior instantly pulled me back a century into the golden age. This hundred-year-old institution was Hemingway's favorite corner—so much inspiration was born right here.

Through the magnificent old city walls, lively flagstone streets welcomed us. I headed straight for the bar street, lined with mouthwatering Basque pintxos—and even found super-affordable Michelin-recommended bites. Yes, Michelin on the Camino. Who says pilgrimage has to mean suffering? Culture, history, and food—nothing is missing here.

In the afternoon I joined a pilgrim guided tour, and luckily the English session ended up as a private tour for just the three of us. Our volunteer guide led us into a church normally closed to the public—many of Spain's early churches were built with gold. Standing inside, the glittering gold felt not gaudy at all, but exquisitely beautiful and serene.

To close the tour, the priest led the pilgrims in a hymn. The moment the choir sang out the sacred melody, the sound echoing through the church, I broke out in goosebumps. In that moment I felt the power of faith—it transcends language and culture, becoming humanity's purest spiritual refuge.

After that stunning choral hymn, body and mind felt thoroughly cleansed.

Back at the stream by the albergue, the sunset's warmth and light were just right. Stepping into the cool river water, watching the sparkling surface—probably the most perfect end to a day.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Pamplona

Pamplona (Iruña) is the capital of Navarre, a city over 2,000 years old. But the world knows it for two reasons:

1. Hemingway—his 1926 novel The Sun Also Rises wrote Pamplona's Running of the Bulls into modern literary history. The book's Café Iruña is still in business today.

2. Running of the Bulls (San Fermín)—every July 6–14, bulls charge through the old town's flagstone streets with runners sprinting ahead. Hemingway called it "the most dangerous party in the world."

The Cathedral (Catedral de Santa María) is Gothic, built in the 14th–15th centuries, with one of the oldest Gothic cloisters in all of Spain.
