Day 1｜1480m, 6,000 Calories, and a Grandmother's Birthday

May 21, 2026 · SJPP → Roncesvalles

Six in the morning, 13-degree air so crisp it woke me right up. A cool breeze carried the smell of coffee and bread.

After the 6-euro breakfast at Gîte BIDEAN, we were glad about the best decision we had made last night: spending 8 euros to forward our unneeded luggage to the next town. Even with fresh legs on day one, this is a 30-plus-day war of attrition—every extra kilo gets magnified tenfold in the days to come.

Through the ancient city walls, we officially stepped onto the Camino Francés. What greeted us was an endless steep climb. But luckily, when the first rays of dawn broke through the clouds, god rays spilled across the valley—as if blessing our group about to cross the mountains.

1,480 meters of climbing. That is not a number—it is every hurried breath. Standing at the foot of the mountain, looking at that steep slope, at that rugged peak disappearing into the clouds, honestly I flinched inside. But once you actually step onto it, you find that however tall the mountain and however treacherous the trail, if you slow your pace and steady your breathing—one step, then another—the mountain path gradually flattens beneath your feet.

Until one moment, when I stopped and looked back at the road I had come—all of it had become scenery beneath my feet. In that moment, what rose in my heart was no longer awe of the mountain, but a deep admiration for that brave self of mine.

As the altitude rose, the trail filled with wild nature. Galloping horses and sheep, even a weathered horse bone—all reminding me of life's fragility and cycles.

On the way, we met VAN from Taiwan, and also an adorable elderly couple. I took an instant photo for them and excitedly shouted "Give me five!"—and they thought I was asking for 5 euros, shaking their heads in fright. Though we barely shared a language, the sincere smiles that dissolved the awkwardness became the clumsiest yet most charming encounter of this stretch.

Near the summit, at my feet I spotted a stone shaped just like Taiwan, with our flag stuck on it. Thousands of kilometers from home, that stone gave me the warmest sense of belonging.

By noon the temperature approached 30 degrees. Past the highest point came this timely water stop. That sip of sweet mountain spring water was nature's finest reward. The chill from the deep mountains not only soothed my parched throat but washed away the heat of a long trek under the blazing sun.

Crossing the highest point at nearly 1,500 meters, we officially stepped out of the French Pyrenees and into Spanish territory. It was not just a crossing on the map, but a shift in mindset.

Entering the dense forest, the trail switched from climbing to descending. The damp, soft leaves made every step feel like walking on cotton, yet my feet found no relief. As the saying goes, "you climb with your lungs and descend with your knees"—the downhill stretch proved it in the most visceral way. My legs, exhausted from the morning's climb, trembled nonstop on the descent, testing the toughness of body and will.

In the afternoon, I finally dragged my trembling legs downhill to the monastery at Roncesvalles. Inside the gate stretched a long queue of pilgrims waiting to check in—remember to book ahead on the official website, because day-one pilgrims almost all stay here, at 14 euros. In the albergue, shoes must be lined up neatly in the designated area to keep the shared space clean and fresh.

When I reached my bunk, I discovered that VAN, the Taiwanese pilgrim I had met on the mountain that morning, was sleeping in the bunk right below mine! That is the magic of the Camino—it always connects people from around the world when you least expect it.

That evening, a 16-euro pilgrim dinner became our salvation. At the table, I learned that a French lady in her sixties sitting with us was celebrating her birthday today. Amid red wine and laughter, I pulled out my instant camera to capture the moment for her—warmth crossing borders and ages, quietly spreading through the laughter.

At eight in the evening, we walked into the thousand-year-old church inside the monastery for the pilgrim mass. When the priest blessed pilgrims from around the world in many languages, solemn and melodious singing echoed off the ancient stone walls, candlelight flickering. In that moment, all the physical exhaustion and mental pressure seemed washed clean, and my heart found a peace it had never known.

Day one: a deep conversation with my body across 1,480 meters of vertical climbing. 30-degree heat and over 6,000 calories burned—this road's first test for us. We were lucky. Just a week before our departure, this very trail had shown its force with a heavy snowstorm that kept three pilgrims on the mountain forever. It reminded us that the Camino never promises smooth sailing.

It is already late May, summer is near—even at ten at night, the golden sunset still refuses to leave. In this long daylight, our story has only just begun. Buen Camino!

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Roncesvalles

Roncesvalles is one of the most legendary places on the Camino. In 778 AD, Charlemagne's rearguard was ambushed here by the Basques, and the commander Roland fell in battle. The battle was immortalized in the medieval epic La Chanson de Roland, the founding work of French literature.

In the 12th century, a pilgrim shelter and church were built here—a "lifeline station" after crossing the Pyrenees. The Church of Santiago inside the monastery is Gothic architecture, drawing countless pilgrims every year.
