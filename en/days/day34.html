Day 34｜Muxía, the Last Day of Walking, and a Conversation with the Asphalt

June 23, 2026 · Fisterra → Muxía · 33km

Last night in Fisterra, we watched the sun sink into the deep sea. Today I shouldered my pack for the final 33 kilometers, heading toward Muxía — the symbol of "cleansing and rebirth". My old soul is left behind at the end of the world; every step forward now is a brand-new me.

Stepping out the door to a gorgeous sunrise over the sea, I got so excited I walked straight in the wrong direction! I found the pilgrim statue I'd passed last night — the wooden staff in his hand had fallen off. Looks like heaven sent me back specially to pick up his staff for him!

Turning around, I actually spotted a familiar Taiwan sticker on the roadside — Taiwanese footprints really are everywhere! And at that very moment, golden sunlight poured down, turning the whole bay into a mirror of gold, with a sailboat gliding silently across the water. This wrong turn at dawn brought an unexpected sunrise and surprise.

Looking at this gorgeous golden coastline at dawn, I was in the best mood! The Camino's yellow arrows were supposed to cut straight across the land here, over the hills toward Muxía on the other side. But I thought: it's the last day — how romantic to walk by the sea! So I opened Google Maps and decisively abandoned the official markers, choosing a route along the coast instead.

At first I still thought I'd chosen right, retracing yesterday's gorgeous beaches and the Fisterra landmark along the way. But once the coastline ended and I followed the navigation inland, the whole picture changed in an instant! Cars kept honking warnings at me, every honk making my heart pound — that's when I realized Google's walking mode had led me onto a highway with terrifyingly fast traffic! But I'd already walked over ten kilometers; turning back was too far, and there was no other road ahead — all I could do was lower my head and hurry forward.

Worse, my water bottle was long empty. For 16 kilometers of highway: no villages ahead, no shops behind, no bars, no water, not a scrap of shade. With cracked lips, under the blazing sun, staring at the endless asphalt, I genuinely wondered if I'd get heatstroke right there on the road.

After surviving the long highway ordeal, I finally crossed the road and found a small supermarket — only in that moment did my whole body truly relax. Back on the trail alone, when the familiar yellow shell symbol appeared again at an intersection, I knew I was finally back on the true Camino. The yellow arrows I'd grown used to seeing every day — reuniting with them after being lost and afraid made me realize how reassuring that guidance really is. Who would have thought: after walking for over thirty days, the day that would carve itself deepest into me would be this very last one!

Arriving in the town of Muxía, the navigation showed the albergue just up the slope off the main road, and I figured the straight line was fastest. Instead I marched myself straight into a desolate patch of weeds — bushwhacking through, the thorny weeds drawing several scratches across my legs, looking like a wilderness survival disaster. The seagulls overhead cried one after another, sounding exactly like they were laughing at my stupidity.

At last I reached tonight's albergue — which looks like a modern design museum! Thinking back on this final day, it really was like a carefully arranged yet completely out-of-control adventure: from the intoxicating coastline at dawn, to the highway scare, to the merciless mockery from the seagulls.

Only after washing off the day's dust did I walk into the town library to collect this official Muxía certificate. From the very first step in France to standing here at the end of the world — over a thousand kilometers, truly walked. This certificate is not just a souvenir of the journey; it's a reminder to myself — I really did it.

With this adventure full of surprises behind me, I ordered a pilgrim seafood feast with sangria to properly reward myself! Before nightfall I climbed to the rooftop and watched the pink sunset afterglow — a whole day of twists and turns dissolving into the calm of this moment.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Rebirth in Muxía

Muxía and Fisterra each have their own 0km marker and arrival certificate — most people walk both. Fisterra symbolizes "death" — saying goodbye in the sunset; Muxía symbolizes "rebirth" — welcoming a new self in the sunrise. Only by walking both ends is the end of the world truly complete.

The Muxía certificate (the Muxiana) is collected at the town library with your credencial. In the evening, don't forget to walk out to the rocky shore to see the church of the boat of Our Lady and the legendary sail stone.

🗺️ Follow the yellow arrows! Google walking mode may lead you onto a highway full of cars.

💧 Fill your bottle before long stretches — shadeless roads are more brutal than you'd think.
