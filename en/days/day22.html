Day 22｜Under 200 km to Go, and the Mystery of the Korean Pilgrims

June 11, 2026 · Ponferrada → Villafranca del Bierzo · 26km 🍇

Day 22. I headed out before the sky was fully light. Ponferrada's Templar castle floated in the dawn glow, quiet to the point of unreality — yesterday it loomed like a movie set, all majesty; this morning it felt gentle, as if seeing us off. I stepped into the just-waking light and kept walking west, officially entering the sub-200-km stretch.

Not long after, a signpost stopped me cold — two meters up, a tiny Taiwanese flag sticker. I stared up at it for ages with exactly one question in my head: how on earth did that get up there? Do pilgrims pack a ladder? Or did someone get lifted up by their buddies to stick it on? Like so many things on the Camino, this mystery will probably never be solved.

Walking on, I looked up to see a familiar espadaña bell wall, a bird perched on top — utterly still, blending perfectly into the ancient stone, looking from afar like a finely carved statue. I watched it for a good while; it didn't move a muscle. Flawless acting. The Camino is like this — always some wonderfully random little highlight that cracks you up for no reason.

Then came the main event — the final 200 km! No official stone marker here; the number is printed right on a roadside bar's advertising board. The owner really understands pilgrims: at this stage of the journey, not stopping in for a drink would feel wrong. Well then — don't mind if I do. Sitting down to celebrate 600 km walked with a coffee (or you-know-what).

Passing the Camponaraya junction, my eyes were grabbed by a weightlifting statue — barbell, Olympic rings, such a commanding shape. I looked it up: this is the hometown of Spanish legendary women's weightlifter Lidia Valentín, an Olympic gold medalist! The little town greets passing pilgrims with an Olympic-rings installation — that kind of local pride is so quintessentially small-town Spain.

Today I also unlocked a new word: "Buen día" — Spanish for "have a beautiful day." I learned it from the albergue host this morning and put it straight to use, greeting everyone I met all day long, every pilgrim I passed. Doesn't matter that my pronunciation is terrible — sincerity scores full marks. A smile back, and the day is worth it. Feel the wind, feel simple joy, keep walking!

The afternoon stretch was criminally beautiful — vineyards on both sides, every shot a postcard. I walked and chatted with the Taiwanese-Australian friend I'd met yesterday, sinking into the pastoral scenery — and the next second got startled into jumping by a dog barking wildly from a second-floor window, half its body practically hanging out. Looked super dangerous and super funny at once. A little fright with a lot of laughter — that's the everyday seasoning of the Camino.

Reaching Villafranca del Bierzo at dusk — tonight's albergue is a storied one: Albergue San Nicolás el Real. The moment I walked in I gasped: a history-soaked stone-arched cloister with a magnificent staircase dome, like sleeping in a palace. After dorm bunks in sports halls on this Camino, a place like this makes you wonder if you walked onto the wrong set.

And the main event is this: this albergue is the filming location of the famous Korean show "Spanish Hostel"! If you've ever wondered why the Camino is always full of Koreans, this show is a big part of the answer — many Koreans first learned about this road from it. My companion joked at the entrance: "Santiago isn't my finish line — THIS is!" The whole group burst out laughing. And with only 10 beds here, getting to see the show's iconic scenes in person AND snag a bed — this trip has more than paid for itself.

At dusk I climbed up high to switch to god's-eye view, looking down over this centuries-old monastery albergue — the quiet town cradled by mountains, the distant castle silhouette, all soaked in golden sunset like a medieval painting. No drone shots in this batch — I'll keep this picture in my eyes instead. Some views don't fit in a camera; you can only remember them with your heart.

Treating myself to a paella for dinner. The moment it hit the table, half of the day's 26 km of fatigue evaporated — saffron aroma with a heap of seafood, on a monastery-town night. That is a pilgrim's simplest happiness. Tonight's protein and carbs are tomorrow's full tank of energy!

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Villafranca del Bierzo

Villafranca del Bierzo is the last major town before Galicia, tucked into the mountain-ringed Bierzo wine region. Its Monastery of San Nicolás el Real is now a pilgrims' albergue, its stone-arched cloister and staircase dome as beautiful as a palace.

It is also the answer to the mystery of the Korean pilgrims — the famous Korean show "Spanish Hostel" filmed scenes here, and after it aired, a wave of Korean Camino fever followed; meeting Korean pilgrims on the road has become part of the everyday scenery. The albergue has only 10 beds, so get there early if you want to walk through the show's iconic scenes.
