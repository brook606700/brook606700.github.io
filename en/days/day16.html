Day 16｜A 72-Year-Old Korean Grandma, the Halfway Marker, and a 40-km Farewell

June 5, 2026 · Carrión de los Condes → Sahagún · 40km

Yesterday I deliberately walked only 24 km — partly to stay at the nuns' albergue, but mostly for today: while my legs were still fresh in the early morning, I set off with 700 ml of water straight into the 17-km stretch with no services.

The sky was still dark, and in the wilderness there was only a single beam of phone light ahead of me. It belonged to a 72-year-old Korean grandma, walking alone. I asked her why she insisted on carrying her big backpack herself. She smiled and showed me a Polaroid photo on her phone — a post with over two thousand likes that read: "Before I leave this world, I want to carry my own backpack and walk the whole Camino." She told me calmly that her husband is gone, but he once walked the Camino, and she insists on carrying the full load so she can truly feel every step he took back then. Only then did I understand: what keeps her walking in the dark was never stamina, but endless love and longing.

The wilderness stretched endlessly before me, the path beneath my feet like a thin line running to infinity. After crossing the no-services stretch, most people stop at the town just ahead — but with the cool, pleasant weather on our side, we decided to push the day's mileage to 40 km! Gaining ground on the schedule also meant saying goodbye to familiar faces I always smiled at — on the Camino, everyone has their own rhythm. Reluctant as it was, we took a group photo, wished each other Buen Camino, and each went on to shine in our own way.

What appeared before us was the Ermita de la Virgen del Puente (Chapel of Our Lady of the Bridge), flanked by statues of a king and a bishop welcoming every pilgrim who makes it here — reaching this spot means the Camino Francés is exactly half done! A mural by the roadside, showing travelers embracing after walking on with heavy packs, felt like the perfect miniature of the Camino itself.

Before I knew it, I was standing at the "Centro del Camino" marker in the heart of Sahagún — the long 800 km already half walked. The red-brick building on the little hill is the pilgrims' sanctuary, where I picked up my very own Halfway Certificate. Back at the albergue, I celebrated by cooking for myself tonight — pan-fried chicken leg steaks, eaten heartily together. That is the most genuine joy on the Camino!

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Sahagún and the Halfway Certificate

Sahagún is considered the geographical center of the Meseta and the halfway point of the Camino. Its Mudéjar brick churches are part of a UNESCO World Heritage site — Islamic craftsmen built Christian churches out of red brick, freezing the meeting of two civilizations into architecture.

The pilgrim office in Sahagún issues the Halfway Certificate (Certificado de la Media Distancia) (€3, including the entrance fee for the visit).

⚠️ Crossing the no-services stretch (17 km with no resupply): carry at least 2–3 liters of water and keep snacks on you

🏛️ Free entry to the Mudéjar churches; San Tirso and San Lorenzo are the most worth seeing

📜 Halfway certificate €3 (entrance included) — get your credential stamped, then pick it up at the office

🥾 40km · the longest day of the whole journey — a day of pushing forward and saying goodbye
