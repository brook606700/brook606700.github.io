Day 24｜Samos, 7 Extra Kilometers, and the Croatian Grandpa

June 13, 2026 · O Cebreiro → Samos · 31.7km

I set off in the dark under the moonlight, saying goodbye to yesterday's mountaintop with the most beautiful sunset. Looking up, a thin crescent moon still hung in the sky among countless stars, and it was so quiet I could only hear my own footsteps — an opening like this fills half of the day's mood right away.

As I walked, the sky started to burn — a wash of orange-red spreading from behind the ridge, clouds dyed gold and crimson, every shot looking like a postcard. Every sunrise is different, yet each one convinces me that getting up early was the right call.

The classic pilgrim statue looked spectacular in the morning light — wide-brimmed hat, backpack, staff, every detail saying "people have walked past here just like this for centuries." Standing beside it, I suddenly felt like a small stretch of that long river of walkers, and it moved me in an unexpected way.

Arriving in Triacastela, I faced the day's big decision — left or right. To the right lies San Xil, the shorter, traditional mountain route most pilgrims take; to the left lies Samos, about 7 kilometers longer, but a flat and beautiful walk along the river valley, crowned by a spectacular thousand-year-old monastery. Did that question even need thinking? I turned left without hesitation — a few extra kilometers for a completely different view!

At last I reached the town of Samos! We gladly walked 7 extra kilometers for the sight before us — the Monastery of Samos, one of Spain's oldest and largest monasteries and a major spiritual stronghold on the Camino. The moment I spotted the SAMOS landmark in the distance, my feet quickened on their own.

The monastery itself was even huger than I had imagined, its stone walls stretching farther than I could see. The moment I stepped inside and looked up at the classical dome, I was floored — ornate carvings, solemn light. You have to stand there in person to feel the power of a sacred place like this.

Beyond the monastery, the most enchanting thing about Samos is this crystal-clear little river. When the slanting sun filters through the trees onto the water, beams of light dancing on the stream, time itself seems to slow down. I zoned out here for a long while and didn't want to leave at all.

And the biggest surprise: by the river I bumped into the Croatian uncle I had shared an albergue with a few days ago! Honestly, this was already the third time today — morning, noon, and afternoon again, as if the whole day had been scripted. In the end he walked straight up to me and asked for a photo, saying I looked like someone from a movie and earnestly naming a Croatian film title. I didn't understand a single word, but it didn't matter — when words fail, smiles work. Some people you never need to plan to meet; the Camino arranges it for you.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Samos

Samos is home to one of Spain's oldest monasteries — the Monastery of San Julián de Samos (Monasterio de San Julián de Samos), founded in the 6th century. Benedictine monks still live here today, and the complex is so large it takes quite a while to walk around it.

From Triacastela there are two routes: the right-hand one via San Xil is shorter, the traditional mountain path most pilgrims take; the left-hand one via Samos adds about 7 kilometers but follows a gentle, green river valley — one of the most beautiful forest stretches in all of Galicia.

🏛️ Monastery guided tour about €5, led by a monk himself, English OK

🌲 The Samos detour adds 7km but is far more beautiful — worth it
