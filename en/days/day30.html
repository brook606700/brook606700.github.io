Day 30｜Negreira, Leaving the Holy City, a Familiar Quiet

June 19, 2026 · Santiago → Negreira · 22km

Day 30 of the Camino! A Santiago morning wrapped in thick fog. But this isn't the finish line yet — I shoulder my pack and keep walking toward the "end of the world."

Leaving the bustle of the holy city behind, the crowds fall away and only quiet remains. Looking back, the cathedral's twin towers float in the mist — the view feels like everything circling back to where I started.

What are we doing in this photo? Snapping branches into walking sticks! A fellow walker I met later on is a seasoned Chinese hiker — her pack weighs under five kilos and she reaches the albergue first every day. But in these final days her shin went inflamed, so painful she had to see a doctor. To ease the load on her legs, we improvised on the spot. This road was never a race — find your own comfortable rhythm, and not getting hurt is what matters.

Passing through the deep forest, I met a group of kids having class — nature is the finest classroom.

Wow, this border collie isn't afraid of heights, posing so cutely on the stone wall — and the next second its bark scared me silly!

Over the long climb, the magnificent Ponte Maceira greeted us: a medieval stone bridge spanning a crystal-clear river, too beautiful to be real. Paired with an ice-cold beer — this is the rhythm the Camino is supposed to have.

Back among quiet towns and green hills — this calm feels so good. The emigrant monument in the square speaks of a choice: to stride forward resolutely, or to be held back by reluctance? Arriving at the albergue, I lucked into a prime window-side bunk! Gazing out at the green that belongs only to Galicia, needing no extra sound — this kind of peace is just right.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Ponte Maceira

Ponte Maceira is one of the most beautiful medieval villages in Galicia, its stone bridge spanning the Tambre River — once a mandatory crossing for pilgrims heading to Fisterra. The bar by the bridge is the classic rest stop — when you're tired, have a drink by the river.

From Santiago, the Camino splits here into the "end of the world" extension: first to Fisterra, symbol of death and farewell, then to Muxía, symbol of cleansing and rebirth — about 90 kilometers in total, most people walking it in 3 to 4 days.
