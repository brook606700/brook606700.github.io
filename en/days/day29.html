Day 29｜Santiago, After 800 Kilometers — Arriving Is Not the End

June 18, 2026 · O Pedrouzo → Santiago de Compostela · 20km

Day 29 of the Camino! Only the last 20 kilometers stand between me and the cathedral of Santiago.

Only in these final days did it truly hit me: this pilgrimage is almost over. By the official guide, walking 20 to 25 kilometers a day, the 800-kilometer Camino Francés takes about 35 days. But we never chased that "standard answer" — loyal to donation-based albergues, walking entirely at our own pace: some days powering through 40 kilometers, other days willfully strolling just 15 to let our bodies breathe.

Only this last stretch to go. The strange thing: after twenty-some days of counting down the markers, asking "when will I ever get there," now that I'm really here my feelings turn complicated — brimming with anticipation, yet flooded with reluctance to let go. This past month, I had grown used to days where the only thing to think about was "keep walking forward."

Two kilometers to go! A delightful surprise: a comic-and-graffiti shop where a donation gets you a gorgeous wax seal of the cathedral stamped in your credencial — a hidden gem for your pilgrim passport, don't miss it!

📍 Wax seal shop location (Google Maps)

When the mellow sound of bagpipes drifted through the archway, when the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela truly filled my eyes — everything suddenly felt unreal. Standing there, the noise seemed to vanish, and only one quiet yet solid voice remained in my heart: "29 days, more than 800 kilometers — I really made it."

Having barely arrived, with no time even to rest, the first thing I did was head straight for the Pilgrim's Office! To claim this deeply meaningful certificate, you now have to take a number first to avoid the long queue.

Collecting the certificate came with a surprise hidden route: because the three of us were traveling together, we were guided downstairs to the group counter; solo pilgrims take a number in the waiting area instead. Who knew picking up a Compostela had its own little strategy guide!

Downstairs, I typed my journey's starting point and distance into the computer, and a month of memories flooded back in an instant. This Latin Compostela was, in the Middle Ages, a true "VIP ticket to heaven" that could wash away sins; for us today, it is less an indulgence and more the finest proof that we never gave up all those days.

After collecting the official certificate, one more hidden gem: the Church of San Francisco prints its own exclusive pilgrim certificate, free for pilgrims who walked the whole way! After 800 kilometers, you have to unlock this honor.

Certificates in hand, we returned to the square for the photo we had waited 800 kilometers to take. Legend says that over a thousand years ago, a miraculous starlight guided people to the tomb of the apostle Saint James, and this place became known as the "field of stars." Ever since, kings, knights, commoners, and even criminals have all walked toward the same place; in the Middle Ages the road was so deadly — bandits and disease everywhere — that the Knights Templar were created to protect pilgrims. Surviving the walk back then was truly a miracle and a blessing.

The bandits are long gone, and the road has become a path to find yourself again. Along the way I met so many warriors of life: young people getting clean, travelers fighting depression, all determined to leave their comfort zones and force themselves, through the hardest walk, to face their deepest fears. Different eras, different reasons — all ending up in the same place. The moment the shutter clicked, this photo was more than a record of arrival — proof that facing the unknown and the pain, none of us gave up easily.

Arriving in Santiago, besides collecting the certificate, you must not miss the cathedral's Pilgrim's Mass! The priest reads out the nationalities and starting points of the pilgrims who arrived in the last 24 hours, and that moment is deeply moving. And the most anticipated moment of all: the legendary Botafumeiro. In the Middle Ages the giant censer was built to deodorize pilgrims who hadn't bathed for months and ward off disease; today you normally only see it when a group pays to sponsor it — fortune smiled on us, and we happened to catch it! Watching the 80-kilo solid-silver censer swing through the air was the luckiest blessing of the whole journey.

We attended the 7:30 pm mass, and stepping out near nine, the golden sunset lit the whole cathedral in gold — the sunlight giving us a warm embrace.

After a day full of emotion, I rewarded myself with steaming-hot Korean food: rice with richly sauced fried chicken — the perfect celebration feast for reaching the finish line!

29 days, more than 800 kilometers. Before setting off I was nervous and uncertain whether I could finish — after all, I had no habit of hiking or running. But the key is opening your heart to everything on the road and finding your own comfortable rhythm: don't get hurt, walk slowly, and you'll get there. Walking on, the answers along the way turned into blisters, heat waves, wrong turns, and red wine, plus unexpected friends; the most touching moments were the guitar and singing at evening prayer that soothed every heart, the big sister still full of fire at seventy, and cooking and dancing like family with strangers in the albergues. When the cathedral appeared before my eyes, I knew I had arrived — a little thrilled, moved, and lucky enough to witness the Botafumeiro. Yet something felt missing? Perhaps because I know: tomorrow I keep walking, on to the end of the world. The journey is not over yet.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Santiago de Compostela

Santiago de Compostela is the finish line of the whole Camino. Legend says the tomb of the apostle Saint James was discovered here, and "Compostela" comes from the Latin for "field of stars" (Campus Stellae). The old town is a World Heritage site, and the Obradoiro square before the cathedral is the full stop of every pilgrim's 800 kilometers.

The Pilgrim's Office (Oficina del Peregrino) issues the Compostela against a passport full of stamps; groups of three or more get a dedicated downstairs route. The Church of San Francisco offers a separate free full-route certificate. The 7:30 pm Pilgrim's Mass is the most popular — whether you catch the Botafumeiro is pure luck.
