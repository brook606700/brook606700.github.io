Day 36｜Madrid, from One Kilometer Zero to Another

June 25, 2026 · Santiago → Madrid · high-speed rail north

After last night's bonfire revelry, today I finally don't have to rush out before sunrise. I hand-wrote my last words of gratitude at the albergue, then strolled once more to the square in front of Santiago Cathedral. Sitting down for a proper breakfast — fried eggs, muffins, toast with beer — the breeze carried the smell of coffee with a hint of farewell. Savoring this lazy pace, quietly memorizing the scent of this city.

Saying goodbye to Santiago, I boarded the high-speed train to Madrid. As the scenery flew backward outside the window, I looked up — the speed kept climbing, and so did the temperature, all the way to 38 degrees! Madrid is clearly in the middle of a heatwave too.

Off the train, I switched straight into Madrid's labyrinthine metro system. As Spain's capital, it runs one of Europe's largest underground networks, over a century old. After a month of the simple life of "just follow the yellow arrows", being dropped back into this underground maze where you'd be lost without navigation felt genuinely strange.

For tomorrow morning's early flight, tonight I'm staying at HOSTELFLY by the airport. This place is so cool — it has its own little open-air pool, and you can look up and watch incoming planes fly right overhead.

Pack dropped, time to explore the city. First stop: the famous Plaza de España — this flamboyant monument honors Cervantes, and Don Quixote on his skinny horse. An old man obsessed with knightly dreams, mistaking windmills for evil giants, charging at them with his lance without hesitation. Even battered by reality, his code of chivalry never wavered. In Madrid, you have to come see this most romantic of foolish knights.

Downtown Madrid is full of surprises — turn a corner and you meet the 2,000-year-old Egyptian Temple of Debod, a gift Egypt relocated here stone by stone to thank Spain for saving its monuments. Standing at Madrid's most beautiful sunset viewpoint, I watched the golden sun sink slowly toward the horizon while everyone raised their phones to capture this priceless romance.

As night fell, Madrid's streets were just getting started. Strolling down Gran Vía — Madrid's Broadway — century-old buildings and neon lights weave together into a dazzling night. Right in the middle of the square, an energetic street dance was underway — a direct taste of Spain's most authentic passion and fire!

Then on to the center of Madrid — Puerta del Sol. This is not only the holy ground of New Year's Eve countdowns but also the "Kilometer Zero" origin of Spain's entire road network. Standing at this origin, I thought back over these thirty-plus days, these thousand-plus kilometers: from one kilometer zero to another. The Camino may be over, but the journey of life is just beginning.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Puerta del Sol and Kilometer Zero

Puerta del Sol is the center of Madrid, and that "Kilómetro Cero" plaque set in the ground is the starting point for all road distances in Spain — an origin in another sense, just like the 0km markers of Fisterra and Muxía. From the 0km at the end of the world to the 0km of Spain's road network, this journey began at an origin and pauses at an origin.

🐻 Must-shots at Puerta del Sol: the Bear and the Strawberry Tree statue (Madrid's coat of arms) and the Kilometer Zero tile.

🚇 Bring an offline map for the century-old metro maze — the yellow arrows won't save you here.
