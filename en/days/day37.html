Day 37｜Home, a Red Banner, Buen Camino

June 26, 2026 · Madrid → Taiwan · heading home

On the way to the airport, I thought back to the nervousness and uncertainty before departure — wondering whether someone like me, who never hikes and never runs, could really finish; and now here I am, sitting on the flight home.

The most important thing this thousand-kilometer Camino taught me is: don't race anyone. Open your heart, accept everything on the road, and find your own comfortable rhythm. Don't get hurt; walk slowly, and you'll get there.

And the answers along the way were sometimes blisters, heatwaves, and wrong turns — but far more often, they were unexpected encounters: the soothing guitar songs at evening prayer, the spirited woman in her seventies, and cooking, dancing, and sharing life like family with a group of people who started as strangers in the albergue. Stumbling onto an ancient army parade and medieval jousting in León, and finally walking into Santiago Cathedral and seeing the botafumeiro swing with my own eyes.

My dad's dream of traveling the world went unfulfilled before he left us. So this time, I carried his share of longing with me to the end of the world. And now, I did it. Dad, the world is big — and together we've seen a little more of it.

The moment the plane touched down, I switched in an instant from the streets of Madrid back to familiar Taiwan. When I walked out the gate and saw my good friends holding an over-the-top red banner, grinning even wider than me — in that instant I suddenly understood: the true finish line is this familiar taste of home, and these familiar people. It feels so good to be home.

I washed off the dust of this adventure with a long-missed Taiwanese banquet, devouring the tastes of home I'd missed so much — this is truly the most luxurious happiness in the world. The Camino is over, but exploring the world never stops.

Buen Camino! Brook, home safe.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Buen Camino

Buen Camino is how everyone greets each other on the Camino — it means "wishing you a good road". At the start it's a blessing, on the road it's encouragement, at the finish it's congratulations. Only after finishing did I understand: it's not just a greeting, but a kind of magic that turns strangers into family. Take these words home, and give them to everyone about to set out.

👋 And you, having read all 37 journal entries, deserve one too: Buen Camino!
