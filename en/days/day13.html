Day 13｜Escape Room, the Highway & Rainbow Clouds — My First Blister in Hontanas

June 2, 2026 · Burgos → Hontanas · 34km

Almost got trapped in the albergue first thing in the morning. A dozen of us stood before a locked side door full of question marks, fumbling in the dark for nearly half an hour before discovering the key button hidden on the right-hand wall. An albergue "escape room" to start the day.

This has to be the most absurd scene of the entire Camino: trying to take a shortcut to catch up with my companions, I followed Google Maps "walking" directions and boldly marched straight onto the highway — leading three foreigners with me. Luckily, a kind taxi driver descended from heaven, packed us in, and drove us back to the correct route. Five extra kilometers of pointless walking — but the driver refused a single cent, leaving only a cool "Buen Camino" behind. That kindness dissolved every shred of frustration on the spot!

From the cathedral under the full moon to a gorgeous sunrise and a miraculous encounter with rainbow clouds — all the absurdity and getting lost on the road were gently soothed by strangers' kindness. Every footprint and every encounter is this journey's most precious harvest.

Many people choose to skip this stretch of the Meseta plateau by bus from Burgos, but I think missing it would be a real shame. Look around: endless wheat fields and horizon, no rolling mountains — only pure wind and your own footsteps. This road does not test your fitness; it tests a heart that can bear solitude. Skip this wilderness, and you miss the deepest conversation with yourself on the whole Camino.

On foot, by bike, even on horseback — everyone has their own Camino rhythm. For the first time on the road, I ran into a "knight pilgrim" in the flesh. That elegance and style felt like time-traveling back to the Middle Ages in an instant — full cinematic effect!

Step by step forward — long hours alone leave the mind exceptionally clear. Then suddenly cheerful songs and laughter ring out beside you, and that pure joy instantly infects everyone around. Solitude and liveliness weave together perfectly on this road — that is the Camino at its most enchanting!

After a full day walking the endless Windows-wallpaper plateau, I finally reached the town of Hontanas. The private albergue at the town entrance is remarkably stylish — with a hidden indoor swimming pool, no less. And tonight's Spanish chicken paella was a revelation — the best of my entire Camino, crowned with unlimited red wine: a paradise-level reward. And then — day thirteen, my first blister officially checked in. For the road ahead: please be kind, let's coexist peacefully!

From above, the sunset dyes the town of Hontanas golden.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: the Meseta and Hontanas

From Burgos to León, the Meseta plateau is the "emptiest" stretch of the French Way — no mountains, no towns, just wheat fields and wind. Not hard on the legs, hardest on the mind; skip it and you miss the chance to talk with yourself. At the end, Hontanas' private albergue has a stellar reputation — its pool and chicken paella are rated top-three of the whole route by many pilgrims.

⚠️ Do not follow Google Maps "walking shortcuts" blindly — you will end up on the highway
🏊 A private albergue with a pool + unlimited red wine is the best reward after walking the Meseta
🥾 34 km + 5 km of pointless detour
