Day 31｜A Schnitzel Bigger Than My Face, and a Man Who Has Walked for Three and a Half Years

June 20, 2026 · Negreira → Santa Mariña · 21km

Day 31 began in the thick fog of Galicia. The morning light barely filtered through the clouds, and everything before my eyes felt quiet and beautiful.

As I walked on, the sun came out. Sunrise broke through the clouds and lit up the stone village below; sunlight streamed through the big trees while thin mist still lingered on the grass — a Galician morning always has surprises.

Out in the Galician countryside, sometimes you have to make way for a herd of cows coming toward you — two of them walked straight at me down a narrow country lane, blocking the whole path, with more presence than anyone.

Up and down all the way, under a blazing sun. I passed a Galician stone waymark — a scallop shell with the yellow arrow, so the direction was right; ahead lay wide-open grassland, and I climbed a steep gravel slope under the scorching sun — this is just an ordinary day in Galicia.

Luckily today's distance was short — 21 km — and I arrived smoothly at tonight's albergue, which is itself a restaurant. I dropped my pack and ate meat and drank big: ham, salad with fried eggs and Galician beer, breaded pork cutlet with red wine. Nothing could be more satisfying — this is absolutely the greatest luxury on the Camino.

A pilgrim walked over and asked me to help him fill his water bottle. As we chatted I learned he was 55, had left everything behind, and had been walking continuously from the Netherlands for over three years to get here. He never stays in albergues — when hungry he eats wild fruit, when tired he sleeps in trees. He has shrunk the entire weight of his life down to the backpack on his shoulders.

He said: "Keep walking, and the heart stays calm." Watching him show off his densely stamped credencial was deeply moving. It turns out some people walk to reach a destination — and some walk so they never have to stop.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Hórreo Granaries

Walking through Galicia, you'll often spot these little raised stone huts by the roadside — the Hórreo, Galicia's traditional granary. Raised off the ground against dampness, with round stone discs atop the pillars to keep rats from climbing up — cute in shape and clever in design. The more Hórreos you see, the deeper you are in the heart of Galicia.

🐄 When you meet a herd of cows, don't rush — walk slowly around them. They're calmer than you are.

🍽️ Albergues with their own restaurants are usually great value — don't miss the pilgrim dinner.
