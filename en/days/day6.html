Day 6｜Wine Fountain, Summer Snow, and Fellow Taiwanese — Estella → Los Arcos

May 26, 2026 · Estella → Los Arcos · 21km

Setting off early again before dawn. We skipped the 7am pilgrim send-off, because today had a more important mission—the most magical, most famous sight on the entire Camino: the Irache wine fountain.

Two metal taps on a stone wall: one pours water, the other red wine, free for all. We arrived too early—or rather, too late in the day's cycle—and only the last few drops of wine remained. I caught them in the pilgrim shell on my pack: just enough for one sip. That first fruity sip of red wine at dawn, drunk from a shell in one go. A ritual reserved for pilgrims, and honestly very cool.

The morning sun had just shown its face. Before the heatwave struck, we seized the day's most comfortable, most precious golden hour.

A donation-based stall by the roadside. Toss in a coin at will—I took a boiled egg for breakfast. The most direct, purest protein refill of the morning.

Wide vineyards began lining both sides—meaning we had officially entered one of Spain's great red wine regions.

A short rest at a mountain Bar. Barely past ten in the morning, and the sun was already unbearably hot. Quick stop for a beer, then back on the road.

It was fluff-falling season. White down blanketed the ground like a dreamscape of snow. Treading this "summer first snow" under 42-degree heat was a perfect optical illusion.

Walking the shadeless plains, a hand-in-hand silhouette ahead caught my curiosity. I hurried up—and found fellow Taiwanese. The two of them supporting each other all the way, the picture was adorable. We chatted as we walked, and before I knew it we had reached the town of Los Arcos. Even better, that night we ended up in the same albergue.

The young host who checked us in loves learning languages from around the world. He even mentioned that snacks gifted by earlier Taiwanese pilgrims were delicious. Hearing that abroad—that is the finest citizen diplomacy.

Tonight's albergue was lovely, with its own cozy little courtyard. After washing my clothes, I discovered this hand-crank spin dryer. A brilliant no-electricity design—truly a pilgrim's blessing.

An evening stroll through town. In wine country, a bar crawl is mandatory. We ordered Spain's must-drink cocktail—sangria. And there I met another tall Taiwanese compatriot, "Xiang." We gathered in front of the bar chatting away, savoring the pure joy of meeting far from home.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Irache Wine Fountain & Los Arcos

The Irache Wine Fountain (Fuente del Vino) is the most magical sight on the Camino. The history of Bodegas Irache traces back to 958 AD, when Benedictine monks founded Navarra's first pilgrim hospital here. From the 12th century, monks began offering free wine to pilgrims—a tradition that continues to this day. Two taps on the wall: one water, one red wine, free 24 hours, one cup per person suggested.

Los Arcos has only about 1,000 residents, yet boasts an astonishing Baroque-facade church, Santa María. In the 12th century it was a border stronghold between Navarra and Castile. From here, the landscape shifts into the La Rioja vineyards.
