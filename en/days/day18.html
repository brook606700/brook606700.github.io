Day 18｜León: A Boxing Champion's Wax Seal, the House of Light, and a Bar Crawl

June 7, 2026 · Mansilla de las Mulas → León · 19km

Saying goodbye to days of endless grassland — the long Meseta plateau is officially over. A relatively easy day today, setting off toward the big city of León under a golden sunrise!

A sign before a small bridge reads: "I can barely go on! Keep going!" — surely the self-pep-talk of someone who was about to collapse. I couldn't help laughing when I saw it, yet somehow it cheered me on too.

Up ahead I ran into a donativo wax seal stand. This time I didn't just stamp my credencial — I unlocked the coolest spot ever: stamped right on the inside of my scallop shell! Even wilder: the man stamping for us turned out to be a three-time Spanish boxing champion! Meeting a champion on a wilderness trail — that contrast is so Camino.

Leaving the wilderness behind for a big city with traffic lights — León. I pushed on to tonight's donativo albergue — same old routine, backpacks lined up in a row waiting for check-in. Bags down, straight into city-exploration mode! The market was great fun, full of secondhand antiques and cheap fruit and cured meats. But no cooking tonight — my Asian stomach had been craving for days, so I headed straight for an all-you-can-eat Japanese restaurant! It's a beloved local spot you can't get into without a reservation, and the tables groaning with sushi and hot dishes, with our plates piled high in happy chaos, were healed by long-missed Asian flavors.

Asian stomach satisfied, off to León Cathedral! On the way through the bar street I ran into all kinds of costumed groups — turns out it's Spain's stag-and-hen party culture: before the wedding, your friends prank you and parade you through the streets in the silliest costume, drinking as you go. Walking along, I got pulled in for photos too!

Soaking up that festive luck, I walked through the buzzing bar street and was floored by León Cathedral — this 13th-century French Gothic church is called the "House of Light," its soaring twin towers and intricate carvings maxing out the solemnity. Step inside and you understand the awe: nearly 1,800 square meters of stained glass surround you, blue, red, and gold light flooding the whole space, calming the soul in an instant. No camera captures even half its beauty. The cloister and ambulatory are a quiet retreat of Gothic pointed arches and medieval carving.

The moment I stepped out of the cathedral I walked into a big scene — fully armed ancient Roman soldiers! The red banners read "LEG-VII GEMINA" — the Roman Empire's Seventh Gemina Legion. León began as this legion's garrison over two thousand years ago, and even its name comes from the Latin legio (legion). A standard-bearer in a boar skin, heavy infantry in scale armor with red shields, backed by the cathedral — an overwhelming presence. Crashing their photo was absolutely the weekend's biggest bonus.

Pocketing that surprise, I went to the church for the pilgrim blessing — they sang a hymn for us on the spot, full ceremony, and I grabbed a third free wax seal at the gift shop on the way! Kicking off with ice cream, then the main event in León: the bar crawl. Order a drink and a plate of tapas comes free; two cocktails plus snacks for under NT$200 — unbelievably cheap. Save room in your stomach for this place! Cheers!

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: León, the House of Light and the Roman Legion

León takes its name from the Latin legio (Roman legion) — two thousand years ago this was the garrison of Rome's Seventh Legion. León Cathedral (Catedral de León) is called the "House of Light" (Pulchra Leonina) — its 1,800+ square meters of stained glass are the finest in Spain, and the moment you step inside, light swallows you whole.

Next door, Barrio Húmedo (the Wet Quarter) is a tapas paradise where a drink comes with free snacks as standard; must-tries are Cecina (cured beef) and Botillo (stewed-meat sausage).

⛪ Cathedral entry €6, pilgrim price €5 — go inside for the stained glass and cloister

🏛️ LEG-VII legion reenactments usually happen on weekends — spotting one is a bonus

🍽️ Wet Quarter bars: free tapas with your drink; two cocktails for under NT$200
