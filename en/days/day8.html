Day 8｜Mirror-Lake Mornings, the Osborne Bull & an Ice-Cold Beer — Logroño → Nájera

May 28, 2026 · Logroño → Nájera · 29km

From Logroño to Nájera. To beat the midday heat, we set off in the dark once again. Starting early is the best strategy for hiking in high temperatures.

I set off a few minutes late and my companions were already far ahead. Luckily I ran into "Sister Fei", an Italian lady. I played tour guide and led her through the maze-like streets back to the main trail. In return, she gave me a pack of candy with Buen Camino printed on it.

Leaving the maze-like city behind, we passed a park lake. I launched the drone to capture the calm morning lake. Like a mirror — peaceful and healing.

On the road I ran into a squirrel. I snapped a quick photo, and on closer look, its face is basically Pikachu. Adorable.

I walked with a young British woman for a while. Language barrier? Phone translator to the rescue. Fresh out of high school, she is taking a gap year to see the world before going back to school. That is the romance of exploring the world young.

A classic Camino sight. Along the fences by the trail, crosses arranged from twigs are everywhere. Each one is a personal mark left by pilgrims who walked here before us.

Topped out at Alto de la Grajera and unlocked a classic landmark: the Osborne bull.

The morning sun was brutal and there were almost no resupply points along the way. Just as my water ran out completely, a donation-based honesty fridge appeared by the roadside. I grabbed an ice-cold beer and downed it in one go — instantly back to full health.

Arrived at a little town by a crystal-clear stream. A long line of backpacks was already queued at the albergue door — the classic Camino battle for beds. I dropped my pack to hold my spot in line, and while waiting for check-in, I went straight to the stream to soak my feet and cool down completely. Ice water first aid, instantly soothing the hiking fatigue.

Bought groceries at the supermarket — another delicious meal. One thing worth mentioning: Spain gets long hours of sunshine, so the fruit is super sweet and cheap — a must-buy. Nearby, a young guy was strumming a guitar with friends gathered around. A few beers and glasses of red wine later, all fatigue was gone. This is the most comfortable kind of night on the Camino.

I had not slept well for a week straight. I thought the albergue snoring symphony was to blame, but it turned out to be the heat! The sun does not set until 10pm in Spain, air conditioning is rare in Europe, and albergues have almost no AC or fans. My ultimate hack: sleep directly on the icy floor, then crawl back into bed when I wake up cold in the middle of the night — insomnia solved. One tip for anyone about to walk the Camino: bring a "clip-on rechargeable mini fan". Physical cooling is king — it solves the heat problem and protects your sleep quality.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Nájera

Nájera sits beneath red cliffs, with a river running through the center of town. Its most famous sight is the Monastery of Santa María la Real — legend says a king discovered a statue of the Virgin in a cave while hunting, so a church was built inside the cave, and it now holds the tombs of generations of kings.
