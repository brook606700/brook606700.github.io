Day 10｜Dawn Light, a Hong Kong Girl & the Cave Church — A Donativo Night in Tosantos

May 30, 2026 · Grañón → Tosantos · 27km

Day 10 on the French Way. My body is starting to settle into the rhythm of this road, and once again I shoulder my pack and set off before sunrise.

Morning light spills across the land — the purest, most beautiful dawn opening a brand-new day.

On the road I ran into the Hong Kong girl I met yesterday. Just days ago she was hospitalized for three days with severe sunburn — but it has not dulled her spirit one bit. She packed up her mood and is striding forward with a smile.

Watching her full of energy, a thought suddenly struck me: traveling at 20 is the romance of exploring the world; traveling at 50 may have less romance and more back pain. Stepping out of your comfort zone to see the world early — so worth it.

This Brazilian uncle is always the first to sprint into the albergue! The same faces pass me every day, and even without a shared language, a smile feels like an old friend. No wonder everyone says you do not need to set out with companions — because all your companions meet you unexpectedly on the road.

The Brazilian uncle gave me a heart in return! The purest chemistry between people — truly beautiful.

Passing through this small town, plenty of pilgrims choose to stop here — after all, on the scorching Camino, who could resist an albergue with a swimming pool?

Murals full of stories are everywhere along the road. Some return here to leave memories; others arrive, create, and set off again — this road is covered in stories of lives.

Arrived in the little town of Tosantos! Tonight's stay is a donation-based albergue. It is not check-in time yet, so everyone lines up their backpacks outside the door to wait, as usual.

My companions lie down under the trees to cool off and catch up on sleep. On the Camino, going with the flow is the most comfortable state.

This albergue looks a bit chaotic. There is only an 82-year-old grandfather and one volunteer, the two of them quietly holding the whole place together.

Hidden in the town is an ancient cave church that is normally closed to the public. Today the mayor came specially to open the door and lead us into this hidden sanctuary.

Everyone split up the work — washing dishes, setting plates, prepping ingredients. A giant pot of traditional Spanish paella, fragrant and spectacular, ready to hit the table!

It actually felt chaotic when I first walked into the albergue, but everyone instinctively pitched in to help the volunteer — cooking the paella, washing the dishes, treating this place like home. At evening prayer, accompanied by gentle guitar, the volunteer had us read aloud handwritten messages left by pilgrims who speak our language. And at the end, each of us received a scallop shell with the volunteer's handwritten blessing. He said this road once treated him with tenderness, and now it is his turn to pass that kindness on to us.

At small donation-based albergues, I always end up leaving a little extra, because I do not want this kind of beauty to disappear for lack of funds. Volunteers come and go, a new batch of faces every week or two — but the tradition of love is preserved intact. These nights together made me truly, deeply fall in love with the Camino. Turns out walking is just the process; these moments of being held by kindness along the road are the soul of the whole journey.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Tosantos

Tosantos' donation-based albergue is one of the most cherished "soul albergues" on the French Way. A tiny albergue like this, held up by just one or two volunteers and an 82-year-old elder, only keeps running because pilgrims take turns helping out and donating. The town's cave church is normally closed; during pilgrimage season the mayor leads the visit, preserving the most original medieval feel.

🙏 When you meet a donativo albergue, please leave a little extra if you can, so the kindness keeps circulating
🐚 The volunteer's hand-blessed shell is many people's most treasured souvenir of the whole trip
