Day 23｜O Cebreiro, the Final Climb — Life Is Like That Too

June 12, 2026 · Villafranca del Bierzo → O Cebreiro · 29km

Day 23: 29 km with 1,085 m of climbing — seeing those numbers before setting off, only two words came to mind: tough battle. This is one of the steepest sections of the entire Camino Francés, the legendary "final climb." I set off in the dark under moonlight, walking forward to the murmur of running water; my headlamp hit the ground and caught a hidden little smile. Okay, Camino, you sure know how to encourage people. Today, I'll fight it out with you.

Much of this road I walked alone, and it could get dull — but stay alert and surprises pop up on their own: deer grazing leisurely on the hillside, whole fields of flowers drenched in morning light. Standing before the 190-km-to-go marker, I felt it deeply — 800 km sounds long, but with every day's footsteps we get closer to Santiago. Officially stepping into Galicia, even the milestone markers carry a different air.

On the road I ran into the Taiwanese-Australian friend again — chatting and laughing all the way, homesickness instantly soothed. I also met a Japanese exchange student walking the Camino for research; no shared language, no problem — translation apps out, and we chatted the whole way. But the higher I climbed, the heavier the trials got — more and more animal droppings by the roadside, the smell enough to send your soul out of your body. Surviving the smell's hell-trial, the view opened up before me: the sun blazes, but the mountaintop wind is unbelievably cool. Life is like that too.

Near the town, bagpipe music suddenly floated out of the woods! It turned out to be an uncle in traditional dress playing by the roadside — like a welcome song arranged just for pilgrims. After climbing nearly 30 km, hearing that, tears nearly fell along with the sweat.

Entering town, this gift shop is an absolute must! With any purchase, the owner boldly gifts you your own national-flag badge — receiving a badge of your own country on the Camino, that feeling of being recognized is hard to describe.

Finally reaching this albergue I both love and hate! The bunks inside are packed insanely tight — turning around is a challenge. A strong reminder to everyone: guard your belongings — my companion's money was stolen right here, and theft here is reportedly routine. Even worse, the showers have no curtains, and my companion ran into foreigners who would "accidentally" walk in. Privacy and safety take a serious hit. ⚠️ Warning: keep valuables on you, shower with a buddy if you can, and consider bringing a big towel as a makeshift curtain.

And yet, this place hides the most unbeatable sunset of the whole Camino. First we went to the church for vespers, received the pilgrim blessing, and got a little stone painted with a yellow arrow — tiny, but heavy in the palm, as if the whole day's road had been folded into it.

Then back to quietly wait for the sunset. When the gorgeous burning clouds set the whole valley ablaze, every ache and every bedraggled moment was redeemed by that sky. Nature's beauty was the perfect ending to today — the final climb. Life is like that too: the steepest stretch holds the finest view.

Looking back at this stone town, a white horse ambles through the streets, cobblestones wrapped in mountain mist, as if time itself moves slower here. After 1,085 m of climbing, seeing this — worth it all.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: O Cebreiro

O Cebreiro is the first mountaintop village after the Camino Francés enters Galicia, at about 1,300 meters, wreathed in mist year-round — known as the "land of fog."

Its most iconic sights are the pallozas (round stone huts) — round stone dwellings roofed with thatch, a pre-Roman Celtic dwelling form. Snuggling inside in winter is warm and magical.

The village's Church of Santa María la Real is one of the oldest churches on the Camino; legend says a Eucharistic miracle happened here in the 14th century, and its chalice motif became the symbol of Galicia.
