Day 32｜Cee, a Seaside Town, and the Comfort of Beer Shrimp

June 21, 2026 · Santa Mariña → Cee · 32km

Day 32 started before dawn, with Galicia completely swallowed by fog. Ahead of me: a brutal 32-kilometer day.

As I walked, the sun gradually showed its face, piercing the low fog — golden light instantly lit up everything before me. Half a golden sun over the misty plain, a traveler greeting the sunrise on the grassy slope, a lone walker in the glowing misty woods — standing on a small hilltop, the view from the ground could no longer hold this scenery, so I handed the perspective to the sky, flying through the clouds to see the true vastness.

By the roadside I found a lone abandoned shoe. Looking at the waymark stone, I simply hooked it up with my selfie stick and set it firmly back on top of the stone — a seamless fit, completely natural.

On the road I met two foreign friends, and we walked and chatted along the way. They said they knew a shortcut that would save some pointless detours, so I decisively followed in their footsteps. As for whether it was actually faster? Read on and you'll see.

The road to the end is a multiple-choice question: Muxía first, or Fisterra? Most people choose Muxía, because it's on the way and saves 3 km. But we chose the more distant Fisterra. This is not just a route decision — it's a matter of spiritual order. Fisterra symbolizes "death" and letting go; Muxía symbolizes cleansing and "rebirth". We deliberately took the long way because we believe: only by saying goodbye to our old selves in Fisterra first can we find our new souls in Muxía.

Saying goodbye to the werewolf statue in the woods, that patch of blue appeared before my eyes with no warning at all. The sea! Starting from the Pyrenees, crossing the entire peninsula one step at a time, today I finally stand here looking at the edge of the land. The end of the world is right in front of me.

The answer is revealed: the so-called shortcut was purely a beautiful misunderstanding. Today I walked a solid full 32 km. But arriving at tonight's albergue — the cozy living room, the memorial wall full of pilgrim stories, and the Taiwan flag on the wall — it was all so beautiful and relaxing.

Pack dropped. Passing the pilgrim landmark in Cee's square, I stepped into those pilgrim shoes bigger than my face — instantly hilarious.

Straight to the supermarket mall. Having finally made it to the sea, how could I live with myself without grabbing some seafood and cooking it up!

The kitchen is mine: beer shrimp, stir-fried beef, pasta in tomato sauce — done in no time. My foreign friends looked on with envious eyes. This lavish dinner was just too satisfying!

Full and happy, I strolled to the sea. The sun was slowly sinking behind the hills, its golden afterglow gently smoothing the waves. Looking down over the quiet bay dotted with fishing lights — the night in Cee, just the right kind of quiet.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Fisterra or Muxía First?

From Santiago to the end of the world there are two extensions: Fisterra and Muxía. Veterans all say the same thing — go to Fisterra first, then Muxía. Fisterra symbolizes "death": saying goodbye to your old self in the sunset; Muxía symbolizes "rebirth": welcoming your new soul in the sunrise. Get the order right, and the whole journey is complete.

🧭 At the fork, look carefully: Fisterra is farther but worth it; Muxía is on the way and saves 3 km.

🍤 By the sea, eat seafood: buy at the supermarket, cook at the albergue — the ultimate pilgrim dinner.
