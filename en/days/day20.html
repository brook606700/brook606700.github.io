Day 20｜Gaudí's Castle, a Guestbook Coincidence, and a Fated Reunion

June 9, 2026 · Hospital de Órbigo → Rabanal del Camino · 37km

Last night's medieval jousting in Hospital de Órbigo was pure madness — warhorses neighing, lances snapping, the scenes still spinning in my head. Still buzzing, I set off in the dark again today — and the distance is no joke: 37 km in one push, my feet barely awake before they have to clock in. Brutal.

Here, it finally sinks in: the long Meseta plains are behind me, and mountain ranges wait in the distance. I set out into the morning light, the dirt path beneath my feet like a golden ribbon running all the way to the horizon. Looking out over that vast, god's-eye view, morning light spilling across the land, my heart fills with freedom and joy — so this is what it feels like to walk out of the plains. Pure exhilaration.

This bronze pilgrim statue by the roadside caught my eye. For centuries, the people walking this road have changed batch after batch — but the tired body and the bliss of that mouthful of water are exactly the same. Too real, this scene. Topping up my water, I keep walking. Day 20, and the first-day novelty has worn off — at the start every road felt new, every church worth a photo, every stranger thrilling. Now I don't need to look for directions; just follow the yellow arrows. With every tourist filter stripped away, when all of this becomes daily life, that is the real Camino.

Into the city of Astorga — famous as the "Chocolate Capital," the whole town drifting with cocoa sweetness. But the real eye-opener came later: the lion statues on the plaza made their grand entrance, and then the majestic Episcopal Palace of Astorga stopped me dead in my tracks. Before building the world-famous Sagrada Família, Gaudí left this visually explosive work here first — the three-dimensional stone carvings on the doors maxed out on detail. That extreme solemnity and shock — you have to stand there in person to feel it.

Breakfast time! I ducked randomly into a bar next to the church, climbed to the second floor, and the view stopped me cold — a gorgeous semicircular window framing Gaudí's dream castle like a painting. Eating breakfast before a VIP-grade private window view, watching sunlight spill across the stone walls — this meal was pure bliss!

Leaving the majestic church behind — but today's biggest shock wasn't any building. On the road I met a Taiwanese couple; chatting as we walked, we got onto our start dates, and I suddenly remembered a line of warmly familiar Chinese I'd seen in the Zubiri albergue guestbook: "The world is vast, we are tiny — grateful for every encounter, every helping hand, every smile. Buen Camino." I shared this with them, and the next second they laughed and said — they wrote it. Who could have guessed that after eighteen days, hundreds of kilometers away, I would meet the very authors of those words! Goosebumps all over me — what are the odds? On the Camino, nothing is coincidence; every encounter means something. Maybe we walk this road not just to reach the end, but to meet people our lives would otherwise never touch.

Chatting and laughing all the way, I finally reached the mountain town of Rabanal del Camino — 37 km of fatigue flooding in the moment I dropped my pack. No solemn evening vespers tonight, but at afternoon tea the priest spoke to us like an old friend: "As long as you keep walking forward and open your heart to everything on the road, everything is for the best." Yes — today's encounter is surely the best proof of those words.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Astorga

Astorga is the Camino's "Chocolate Capital" — it grew rich on the 19th-century cocoa trade, chocolate scent still drifting through town, with mantecadas and chocolate the must-buy souvenirs. Its most famous landmark is the Episcopal Palace (Palacio Episcopal) by Gaudí — before the Sagrada Família, Gaudí left a fairytale castle here: grey-white granite walls, spires and a moat, angel stone carvings. Standing there in person will genuinely take your breath away.

But my Astorga memory is that guestbook coincidence: a line of Chinese I read in the Zubiri albergue 18 days ago, meeting its Taiwanese authors hundreds of kilometers away 18 days later. The world is vast, we are tiny — but grateful for every encounter, every helping hand, every smile. Buen Camino.
