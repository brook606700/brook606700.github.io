Day 14｜A Monastery at Dawn, an 18% Climb & a Police Stamp — A Birthday by the Pool

June 3, 2026 · Hontanas → Boadilla del Camino · 31km

Before dawn, I set off from the small town in the dark. Out in the wilderness the temperature was only 8 degrees, and just a few headlamps flickered faintly in the darkness along the whole Camino.

As the sky broke into dawn, these 14th-century San Antón monastery ruins appeared — a monastery hospital that once saved countless patients, still keeping its most original candle-lit, electricity-free albergue after six hundred years. Pure medieval charm.

Passing through the archway, the sunrise bloomed brilliantly over the horizon — vast, rolling golden waves of wheat. That ultimate pre-dawn shock is the finest reward for pilgrims who set out in the dark!

A ruined castle wall stands alone on the hill, witness to a thousand years of war and prayer. There it stands between blue sky and wilderness, quietly watching over the Camino at its feet.

Leaving the small town, the Meseta plateau's "final boss" looms ahead — Alto de Mostelares. What looks like gentle wilderness suddenly rears up into a brutal near-18% climb!

Inside the heavy breathing hides the purest conversation with myself. However steep the road ahead, every look up brings a brand-new view; and before I know it, I am already standing high above — that joy of conquest is irreplaceable. As long as your feet keep moving, the cool wind at the summit will carry away every ounce of fatigue.

Standing at the highest point of Alto de Mostelares, the world opens wide before you. As the drone rises, we officially switch to God's-eye view. Watching the white-ribbon slope I just fought my way up, and the endless golden land — against such vastness, every worry shrinks to nothing, leaving only unprecedented calm and freedom.

Looking down at this endless woven green below, I open my arms into the ultimate freedom of this moment. The world is so vast — here I quietly feel it, feeling the wideness and calm inside.

A tip from friends ahead: "There's a police car where you can get a stamp!" I sprinted down the mountain without a second thought. By the time I reached the bottom, the patrol car was about to drive off — but the officer, seeing me sprinting for my life, actually stopped, got out again, and stamped our credentials with a smile! The most hard-core police stamp on the whole Camino: unlocked.

This donation-based massage stop by the roadside. One massage runs at least half an hour, and most pilgrims rushing to make miles just glance and hurry past — but I chose to stop. Shoes and socks off, lying on the massage bed under the green shade, I drifted off in the breeze and felt completely healed. On this long, long road, staying in your own comfort zone matters most. It was never a race — listen to your body, follow your own rhythm, and the long trek becomes a landscape that truly belongs to you.

On the road I met a special mother and son. The little boy, about ten years old, hopped along with his backpack — with even more stamina than the adults! Watching a mother lead her child through the endless wheat fields — this has to be the coolest, most hard-core adventure of his childhood!

Through the endless, poetic golden waves of wheat, I arrived at Boadilla del Camino. Tonight's stay is a private albergue built by an artist. The owner turned the courtyard into a vibrant secret garden, with a small azure pool mirroring the blue sky! Jumping in to ice my feet, feeling the breeze in the tree shade — the perfect ending to today.

Besides the garden pool, you absolutely cannot miss the pilgrim steak special here! Beef so tender and juicy it still makes my mouth water.

Learning that a companion we kept bumping into has his birthday today, everyone built a human pyramid by the pool to celebrate him — leaving the best, most unforgettable mark of youth on the Camino for each other!

Fourteen days of walking, and my body has long settled into the rhythm of setting off in the dark before dawn. Blisters on my soles too — but I am glad I stepped across this wilderness one step at a time, every day like walking inside a real-life Windows wallpaper. Many choose to bus past the Meseta. What it tests was never fitness, but a heart that can bear solitude. The Brazilian uncle who gave us his heart, the volunteers who led us cooking paella, the driver who carried us off the highway for free, the blood-pumping sprint after the police car, the dawn breaking over San Antón monastery, and the crazy human-pyramid birthday by the pool — kindness on this road always shows up right when you need it. Walking is just the process; what moves you most on the road will always be the "people" — that is the Camino at its most enchanting!

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Castrojeriz and the Police Stamp

Castrojeriz and Alto de Mostelares form the steepest part of the Meseta — after the 18% climb, a 360-degree panorama with no rivals. The police-car stamp on the way down is a bonus surprise; Spanish police are very kind to pilgrims, so do not hesitate to ask politely if you meet them.

👮 If you spot a patrol car, chase it down boldly for a stamp — the most hard-core stamp of the whole trip
💆 The donativo massage stop is worth a half-hour pause — listen to your body
🏊 The artist's albergue and steak special in Boadilla are the Meseta's reward
