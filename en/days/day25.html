Day 25｜The 100km Marker and a Journey I Am Not Ready to End

June 14, 2026 · Samos → Portomarín · 38km

I left the town of Samos in the dark and slipped into a pitch-black forest trail — you really need nerves of steel. A few stray dogs suddenly rushed out barking wildly, but luckily we were walking together, and the dogs didn't dare come close to more than one person. That's when I deeply felt how important companions are — wide awake from fright before dawn.

Today is day 25: 38 kilometers with 718 meters of climbing, not easy at all. But by this point the question is no longer "can I walk to Santiago" — it's "after I reach the finish line, then what?" This road has become my daily life, and watching the remaining distance count down, I've started to dread it ending.

Around a bend I ran into a Taiwanese big sister from last night's albergue at a corner café — reuniting in a foreign land is pure joy! Age is truly just a number for her: in her seventies and still full of energy. She laughed that the hardest part of this trip wasn't the walking at all, but traveling this far alone for the first time and sorting out the flights and transport herself — and somehow she has already walked nearly 700 kilometers! Carrying that admirable energy, we chatted our way into the big stop of Sarria.

The moment you step into Sarria, you can feel the whole road suddenly turn "lively"! Many people start here on purpose, tackling only the final hundred kilometers. Why a hundred? Because that's the minimum threshold for the traditional pilgrim certificate (Compostela): 100 kilometers on foot, 200 by bike, with at least two stamps a day. This isn't some souvenir you can just buy — it's an official certificate in Latin proving you truly walked to Santiago on your own two feet. For many, the stretch from Sarria is a dedicated "certificate-unlocking journey"!

Looking at the 100-kilometer marker, I felt surprisingly calm. At the start I was afraid I wouldn't finish; now I only feel proud — I had no idea I had come this far. Only the last hundred kilometers to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela!

Following the signs onward, we finally reached today's big stop, Portomarín. The magnificent long bridge spanning the river looked like a painting. But to truly enter the town, we first had to climb the historic, thigh-testing "hero slope" of stone steps — the day's final trial!

After 38 kilometers in a single day, I stood before the Church of San Nicolás in the town center. This church was once submerged underwater — the locals dismantled it brick by brick, moved it to today's higher ground, and reassembled it, nothing short of an architectural miracle! Don't forget the evening prayer service, and collect its beautiful exclusive stamp.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Sarria and Portomarín

Sarria is the unofficial starting point of the final 100 kilometers. To earn the Compostela certificate you must walk at least 100 kilometers (200 by bike) with at least two stamps a day — so many people fly in and start in Sarria, walking only this last stretch. From here the road turns busy and crowded.

Portomarín is a town that drowned and rose again. In the 1960s, when a dam was built on the Miño River, the entire old town was flooded by the reservoir. The most important church, San Nicolás, was dismantled stone by numbered stone and reassembled on higher ground. Walking inside the church you can still find the numbers from those days; when the reservoir level drops, the ruins of the old town emerge from the water.
