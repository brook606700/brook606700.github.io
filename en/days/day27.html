Day 27｜Melide Octopus, World Cup Night, and Knowing When to Stop

June 16, 2026 · Ligonde → Melide · 25km

Early in the morning, the volunteers prepared a warm breakfast — with a handwritten card for each of us on the table, every line full of blessings. I set off carrying that kindness with me, my heart genuinely warm.

On the road, you can even see pilgrims sleeping rough right on the church steps — that is the Camino! Sleep wherever you end up; going with the flow is the best plan.

On the long pilgrimage, healing hidden characters always appear: a simple guitar and singing that gently catches every exhausted pilgrim.

Inside the final 100 kilometers, I kept meeting group after group of young students. Watching their energetic backs walking side by side, I felt the Camino teaching them, through the purest exhaustion and sweat, the resilience and companionship no classroom could — grit and staying together. Following their pace, my mindset turned young again, and I couldn't help running along, rediscovering that purest joy and freedom!

Plans on the Camino never survive contact with reality! I meant to leave Melide right after lunch, but the owner warmly recommended a gigantic fresh octopus too tempting to pass — and more importantly, tonight is Spain's opening match at the World Cup! Decision made: I'm not walking on. Tonight I stay, soaking up the most electric football atmosphere in a Spanish bar.

I switched to a local bar and watched Spain's opener with a whole room of people! The match on screen was intense, paired with ice-cold drinks and free bar snacks — the score stayed tight, but the whole bar was buzzing! Unlocking this kind of red-hot local experience on the pilgrimage was so worth it!

Hard-earned warning: never pay cash at the bar! Otherwise you'll get back a whole fistful of the smallest coins — enough change to make you cry!

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Melide

Melide is the octopus capital of Galicia. Pulpo a la gallega (Galician-style octopus) is the soul food here — served on a wooden plate with olive oil, paprika, and coarse salt. Just four ingredients, yet hailed as one of Spain's finest seafood dishes. The octopus is boiled in a huge copper pot, snipped into thin slices with scissors, and served on the wooden plate.

Recommendations: Pulpería Ezequiel and Pulpería A Garnacha, the two names every pilgrim agrees on. Pair with the local Ribeiro white wine — a perfect match.

🐙 Pulpo a la gallega about €12-15 per plate, one plate feeds two

⚽ On a match night, don't rush on — the bar atmosphere is priceless
