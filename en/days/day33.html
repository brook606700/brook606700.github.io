Day 33｜Fisterra, the End of the Earth, 0.00km

June 22, 2026 · Cee → Fisterra · 21.7km

Daybreak. I set off along the calm coastline, golden sunlight shimmering on the sea — and today, my destination is the end of the world.

I naively assumed the walk to the end of the world would be a flat, romantic coastline all the way. In reality: another honest-to-goodness mountain climb — even the iron arch cross of San Roque seemed to stand on the mountaintop cheering us on.

Over the hill, the view opened up all at once. Fine white sand underfoot, Atlantic waves before my eyes. An unobstructed, magnificent bay: white sand, a sea breeze, the crystal-clear blue of the Atlantic, the sound of the surf — every step bringing me closer to the end of the world.

Passing this flamboyant, lovely shell house, I was instantly drawn in — built from thousands of scallop shells packed tightly together, even the mermaids and seahorses covered in shells.

For a moment I nearly walked right past the Fisterra landmark! It just stands quietly on the hillside beside the Camino, big white letters against an unbeatable bay — a photo stop before the end of the world.

Coming out of the trail into Fisterra's harbor roundabout, I met this emigrant monument — honoring the countless Galicians who sailed from here into adventure, toward the unknown ocean.

Arriving in the town of Fisterra, the stamps in my credencial prove I walked all the way here — and earn me this Fisterra arrival certificate, free of charge!

In town I had the number-one-rated burger, then stepped into the old church for the evening prayer mass. All of this was preparation — so that at sunset, I could walk the final mile to the end of the world!

Past 8 in the evening, we set out on the final mile to the end of the world. Fisterra is Latin for "the end of the earth". In the minds of ancient Europeans, this was the edge of the world — the sun sank into the sea here every day, with no land beyond. It symbolizes "death and endings".

Finally, on our own two feet, we reached this milestone: Km 0.000. If Santiago is the destination of the journey, then standing on the cliffs at the end of the world, the feeling inside is even more overwhelming.

That bronze pilgrim boot on the rock is engraved with the sweat and scars of countless feet; before the cliffside cross, a sea of clouds slowly churns, and heaven and earth freeze completely before my eyes. Everyone who reaches this place just gazes quietly at the vastness of the Atlantic, letting the sea wind wash away every attachment.

Where the land completely disappears, I look down from the clouds upon this end of the earth. Ancient walkers reached the edge of the ocean and let the sea wind wash away a whole body's worth of dust, letting their old souls sink into the deep with the sunset. Lay down every burden and attachment — only by experiencing death at the end of the world can you greet true rebirth in tomorrow's dawn.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Fisterra

The name Fisterra comes from the Latin "Finis Terrae" — the end of the earth. Standing on these cliffs, the ancient Romans watched the sun vanish into a horizon with no land at all, convinced they stood at the edge of the world.

Pilgrims here keep three traditions: burning a pair of shoes or a piece of clothing (the death of the old self and rebirth), watching the sunset by the sea (washing away the dust of the road), and collecting the Fisterrana certificate (free with your credencial stamps, proof that you walked to the end of the world).

📜 Pick up the Fisterrana at the pilgrim office in town — free with your credencial.

🌅 The wind by the lighthouse cliffs is fierce — stand steady before taking that sunset photo.
