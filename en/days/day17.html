Day 17｜36 km, Sunrise, and a Chinese Mini-Lesson

June 6, 2026 · Sahagún → Mansilla de las Mulas · 36km

I kept walking in the dark this morning, aiming straight for Mansilla de las Mulas, nearly 40 km away. The reward for pushing through the dark is greeting the sunrise — watching the sunlight slowly light up the whole Camino, a sight I never tire of.

And then — the Hong Kong girl again! Walking too far a few days ago hurt her ankle, but she hasn't given up; she's still moving forward at her own pace. On the Camino, everyone faces their own trial — a simple farewell, leaving my blessing behind, is the best encouragement I can give.

After pushing nearly 36 km to the albergue, today's highlight turned out to be this passionate volunteer — he is crazy enthusiastic about learning Chinese and turned check-in into a Chinese mini-lesson on the spot! Notebook in hand, he copied down words one by one and repeated after us: "nǐ hǎo," "xiè xie," "jiā yóu." His earnestness made me forget the blisters on my feet and the heat rash.

The town keeps its medieval cobblestone walls, an eye-catching red-and-white church, and finally the pilgrim monument — looking at the statue slumped on the ground felt like looking at myself after two straight days of nearly 40 km. That is me, exactly!

Two days in a row above 35 degrees: one blister has become two, and heat rash has broken out on my arms. But it's already day 17 and this is only just showing up — later than for most people — because I walk inside my comfort zone every day: no forcing it, no showing off, just listening to my body's voice, and the long trek becomes scenery of my own.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Mansilla de las Mulas

This stretch from Sahagún toward León is widely considered the most monotonous of the whole route — a dead-straight dirt track, wheat fields on both sides, not a scrap of shade. But it is also on this stretch that pilgrims learn the difference between "walking" and "hiking." Hiking is moving; walking is meditation.

Mansilla de las Mulas preserves its medieval walls and gates; the red-and-white church stands out strikingly among the wheat fields, and the pilgrim monument at the town entrance honors the countless travelers who have passed through.

🫧 Blister care: sterilize a needle, prick a small hole at the edge to drain, keep the skin, cover with Compeed

🌡️ Heat-rash prevention: breathable clothing + mandatory midday rest + a wet bandana to cool down

💊 Rub Vaseline on your feet before setting off — it prevents 80% of blisters
