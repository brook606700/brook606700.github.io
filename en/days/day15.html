Day 15｜Flow, Scallop Shells & a Nun's Heart Charm

June 4, 2026 · Boadilla del Camino → Carrión de los Condes · 24km

Once again I shouldered my pack before dawn, walking into the morning light. A pilgrim silhouette by the roadside captured this road perfectly — the "scallop shell" on the chest symbolizing all routes converging at one destination, the "stars" recalling that Santiago means "field of stars".

Step after step, everything around me seemed to fall silent. Once in flow, walking is no longer a drain on the body, but a deep moving meditation.

To stay at the nuns' albergue, I deliberately shortened the day to 24 km, arriving early to secure my spot. At just 10 euros, the nuns' albergue earns full marks for its stone walls, wooden beams, and the taste of history.

The most unforgettable moment was receiving a hand-knitted heart charm from the nun at evening prayer — that warm blessing reserved for pilgrims gave this quiet day its gentlest ending. Not every day needs a spectacular story. Finishing a day quietly, on foot, is itself the most complete harvest.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Carrión de los Condes

Carrión de los Condes takes its name from the 10th-century counts (condes) of Castile — a major political center in the Middle Ages. Above the door of Santa María church, a carving shows pilgrims kneeling before the Virgin: a medieval blessing for pilgrims.

There is a famous saying here: "De Carrión a Sahagún, solo campo y sol." — From Carrión to Sahagún, only fields and sun. The stretch ahead is the longest resupply-free section of the whole route (about 17 km), so carry enough water.

⚠️ Carrión → Sahagún: 17 km with no villages at all — carry at least 2 liters of water

⛪ The Espiritu Santo convent albergue costs just 10 euros — the nun's heart charm is a limited-edition tenderness

🧘 Flow is not zoning out, it is the deepest focus — walk on, and the world narrows to the sound of footsteps

🥾 About 24 km · a deliberately slowed-down day, for a place worth lingering
