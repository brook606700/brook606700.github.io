Day 11｜Paw Prints, a Wax Seal & NT$1,000 Wool Socks — The Breathtaking View from the Top

May 31, 2026 · Tosantos → Cardeñuela Riopico · 23km

Just out of the albergue, a kitten blocked my path begging for pets. I stood no chance against the charm offensive — I crouched down and enjoyed it properly. It even gifted me a one-of-a-kind Camino souvenir: a paw print.

A crowd suddenly gathered ahead. Walking closer, I found everyone queuing for a special donation-based wax seal. Sealing an exclusive memory into my credencial — ritual level maxed out. The quiet morning town looked like a painting through the drone lens.

Then I ran into the uncle I once climbed a mountain with in the dark. No words needed — one high-five, as happy as long-lost best friends.

Into the forest I went. The trees blocked the blazing sun, but not the ache in my legs. This up-and-down mountain road became the most honest conversation with myself. The process was exhausting, but the grounded calm earned with every step is irreplaceable.

Friends on the road were amazed at my pace today. To make it to the End of the World on my own two feet, I smashed straight through my comfort zone of about 25 km a day and stretched the distance decisively. Over the ridge and into a small-town bar — food and drink mode on. A big gulp and a rest after burning through all my energy — unbelievably satisfying.

Then I noticed: these NT$1,000 WOAWOA wool socks have carried me through 10 hard days. They may not last forever, but not a single blister. Retired with honor today.

After passing through the small town of Agés, another climb greeted me. High noon, blazing sun and shimmering heat, dueling my fitness on a rugged gravel slope. At the top stands a cross, and the sign beside it reads: "Since crossing the Navarre mountains, never have we enjoyed such a beautiful view." Standing on the summit overlooking the endless plains, it all turned into the most grounded kind of awe.

Finally reached the albergue! Tomorrow, on to the big city of Burgos.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Agés and the Summit View

The steep slope after Agés is the last climb before Burgos. The cross at the top carries the line "the most beautiful view since Navarre", photographed by countless pilgrims as a keepsake. The descent leads into gentle slopes and forest trails — slow down, and treat yourself to a well-earned beer.
