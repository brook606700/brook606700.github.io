Day 0｜Finally Here, Setting Off Tomorrow

May 20, 2026 · SJPP

Finally here.

From familiar Taiwan, across exotic Abu Dhabi, I finally landed in romantic Paris. These thousands of kilometers of flights and transfers were all for one thing: the first step tomorrow morning.

Before departure at Taoyuan Airport, my friends surprised me with a warm group hug. It was not just a blessing—it felt like they packed their love and longing into my bag to travel with me.

Etihad Airways turned out to be surprisingly pleasant. The ground staff were very lenient with carry-on luggage, which was a relief for backpack-only me. On board I met a fellow Taiwanese traveler also flying to Paris, and even learned about a hidden perk: free transit accommodation. But the excitement of arriving in a foreign land was quickly pulled back to reality by the endless immigration queue at Paris airport. After two hours in line, I finally understood why veterans say to leave four hours for connections.

After meeting up smoothly with my first companion KIKI, we took a fixed-price 65-euro taxi into the city, narrowly avoiding being lured into a metered cab. Then the TGV high-speed train carried us through the poetic French countryside. Watching the Louvre and the Eiffel Tower glide past on Google Maps, I made a mental note: if friends ever ask me to plan a Camino for them, I really should tell them to stay a few extra days in Paris. But right now, sitting in the single seat I had booked three months early, I watched a cute foreign kid next to me staring curiously, handed him a freshly printed instant photo, and seeing his smile, the scenery outside the window somehow gained an extra memory.

With the arrival of my second companion Jamie, we reached the true starting point of this Camino Francés—Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port (SJPP).

Walking the worn stone steps of this ancient town, I arrived at the Pilgrim's Office. The moment the first stamp landed on the pilgrim passport I had bought for 3 euros, the surreal feeling slowly settled into something real. In the donation area I picked a scallop shell. Its pale ridges look like countless rivers—symbolizing how all of us, from all over the world, carry different stories yet all flow toward the same sea, the same destination: Santiago.

In the evening, my last companion Cindy joined us. We had booked our stay in town back in Taiwan, and were lucky to be assigned an independent four-person room. At 20 euros for the night it was a bargain. Even in high season, restaurants in this little town mostly close by seven or eight! We found a vending-machine pizza for 10 euros with a strange taste—to be honest, it was pretty awful.

But in the moment the setting sun dyed this ancient town gold, I felt a calm I had never known before.

Tomorrow, I will cross the Pyrenees. It is the hardest day of the whole journey—but right now, I just want to quietly enjoy this little town's sunshine and the sound of the wind.

See you tomorrow.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: SJPP

Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port (SJPP) means "Saint John – foot of the mountain – mountain pass" in French. In the 12th century, this was the Kingdom of Navarre's military fortress on the northern side of the Pyrenees, and its red walls and castle still stand on the hillside today.

Since the 17th century, SJPP has been the traditional starting point of the Camino Francés. Pilgrims from all over the world set out from here, crossing the Pyrenees on an 800-kilometer journey.

🍽️ Must-eat: Basque cake (Gâteau Basque), local Ossau-Iraty cheese

>

⚠️ Note: accommodation is extremely hard to find in high season (May–September)—book ahead! Almost all restaurants in town close after 8pm.
