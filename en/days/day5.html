Day 5｜Starry Skies, a Korean Grandma, and 100 Kilometers — Puente la Reina → Estella

May 25, 2026 · Puente la Reina → Estella · 22.5km

Setting off in the dark again today. At Puente la Reina I met a sky full of stars, set up my camera for a time-lapse—and turned around to find my companions had walked far ahead.

Walking alone in the dark, I reached a fork and happened to meet an elderly gentleman. We teamed up and chose the mountain route, looking out for each other on the pitch-black trail. That wordless companionship is this journey's most precious gift. Sometimes the deepest connection needs no words at all.

After climbing in the deep mountains for over an hour, I finally crossed the highest point. Today did not start easy at all.

On the trail I met a 72-year-old Korean grandma walking the Camino alone. We shared no language, but her cheerfulness and courage were utterly contagious. Her smile proved it: age is never the limit—mindset is. She showed with her own steps what "it is never too late" really means.

The countryside on this stretch was unbeatable. Olive groves, vineyards, and wheat fields woven together—this is the classic Navarra landscape. As my drone rose, the quiet of the morning town unfolded beneath me.

This route had something special: reaching a town, the trail led straight through a house. Stop when you pass—there is a stamp inside you must get!

Threading through a medieval town. Stone churches, narrow lanes, fountains—every turn rewinds time. Few people on the trail, my own rhythm to keep, enjoying a solo walk!

Passing an unattended donation-based fruit stand—no owner at the stall, just the purest trust between people.

By ten in the morning the sun hung high, roasting everyone—and our on-foot mileage officially broke 100 kilometers. The test was long past physical: do not look at how far the finish line is, focus only on the step in front of you. Stay in the present, and the goal will arrive on its own.

When we finally arrived in Estella, the relief was beyond words. Estella means "star" in Spanish, and its hospitals, churches, monasteries, and bridges were all built to serve pilgrims. Walking these ancient flagstone streets, I felt the weight and warmth of history—and the footprints and blessings of countless pilgrims left on this road.

Today's home base is the Estella public albergue in the town of stars. Just 8 euros a night! Spacious, fitting tons of pilgrims, and the whole place was impeccable. Value for money: full marks.

With the weekend on, the 12th-century Romanesque church of San Pedro de la Rúa was locked tight. So off to a town restaurant for a pilgrim menu: starter, steak, plus an ice-cold beer—instantly back to full health.

By noon the temperature soared to 42 degrees—the heatwave was no joke. Lucky that we had stuck to setting off in the dark these past days, dodging the worst hours.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Estella, City of Stars

Estella simply means "star" in Spanish. In the 11th century, the Navarrese king Sancho Ramírez founded the city to serve pilgrims—its hospitals, churches, monasteries, and bridges were all born for pilgrims. Known as the "Toledo on the Ega river," it holds a wealth of Romanesque churches and palace ruins.
