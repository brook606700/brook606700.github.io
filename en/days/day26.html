Day 26｜Texas Volunteers, Handwritten Postcards, and Feeling Cared For

June 15, 2026 · Portomarín → Ligonde · 16.5km

It poured last night and I went to bed a little worried — but the morning greeted us with something beautiful! Today's plan is pure bliss: from Portomarín to Ligonde is only 16.5 kilometers. Why so short on purpose? Because tonight I'm staying at the last donation-based albergue on the entire Camino! Time to slow down and savor a rare, lazy day.

I've seen plenty of pilgrims walking with dogs, but this pair stopped me in my tracks: the dog can only move its front legs, its hind legs supported by a little wheelchair. I don't know how far they've come — I only saw that when it stops, its owner stops; when it moves forward, its owner walks beside it. Maybe where the finish line is doesn't matter on the Camino; the most precious thing is this never-leaving-each-other companionship.

Walking along, a familiar figure appeared ahead — the Korean girl I had met at the Cruz de Ferro! The Camino is like this: people you're meant to meet keep crossing your path again and again on this road.

Slowing down really did bring a lovely surprise: here the stamp isn't stamped at all — it's drawn by hand, stroke by stroke. It must be the most one-of-a-kind souvenir of the whole pilgrimage!

Around the corner, the place with open arms for every pilgrim is tonight's donation-based albergue. I deliberately walked short for this special warmth and grabbed one of only 7 beds — absolutely worth it!

The wooden sign is adorable: "If your heart is full of gratitude, ring this bell!" Being welcomed into an albergue so full of human warmth, my heart held nothing but gratitude — I rang the bell without hesitation, telling them with its chime: yes, we're here!

I spontaneously asked this group of Texas volunteers to dance, and they picked it up in under 10 minutes! They're natural-born dancers — full marks for rhythm and passion!

The volunteers here are so full of love! For everyone's hygiene, they collect backpacks into boxes for thorough disinfection — and after disinfecting, they carry your impossibly heavy pack upstairs for you! This selfless, warm-hearted service honestly moves you to tears.

Ta-da, finally checked in! The cozy wooden loft holds only 7 beds, and being lucky enough to be one of the seven feels pure happiness.

Quietly listening to a fellow pilgrim play and sing, simple melodies echoing through the stone house — my most blissful, most relaxed moment.

At dinner, everyone gathered around the long table in the garden for pasta and red wine thoughtfully prepared by the volunteers. Pilgrims from around the world chatted like family at that moment — this pure sharing and warmth is the most enchanting part of a donation-based albergue.

After dinner, dessert time: everyone picked the photo that best captured their mood. I chose this "dancer in the rain" shot — no matter what comes your way, just enjoy the moment.

📚 Pilgrim's mini-guide: Ligonde

Ligonde is a tiny village with almost no presence after Sarria, but its quiet is exactly what the final 100 kilometers need most. After the 100km marker the crowds swell, and a small village where only a few stop becomes a true refuge — and its donation-based albergue is the last one on the entire Camino Francés, kept alive purely by volunteers' love.

Donativo doesn't mean free — it means pay what your heart says: lodging, dinner, and breakfast are all run by volunteers, and what you put in is passed on to the next pilgrim. This chain of trust has walked on for centuries, more precious than any five-star bed.
